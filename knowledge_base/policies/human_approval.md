# Human Approval Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines when Human-in-the-Loop validation or approval is mandatory.

The assistant can analyze evidence and recommend diagnostic actions. Responsibility for operational, financial, security, configuration, and approval decisions remains with authorized human personnel.

## Mandatory Human Validation

Human validation is required when any of the following conditions applies:

### Insufficient Evidence

- no reliable knowledge-base evidence was retrieved;
- the available evidence does not support a diagnostic hypothesis;
- required SAP context is unavailable;
- the ticket remains materially incomplete after clarification.

### Conflicting Evidence

Human validation is required when approved sources, SAP context, or other evidence provide materially conflicting guidance.

The assistant must expose the conflict and must not select one interpretation without supporting evidence.

### Low Confidence

A LOW application-calculated confidence level requires human validation.

The LLM must not override or recalculate this decision.

### State-Changing SAP Actions

Human authorization and execution are mandatory for actions that change SAP state, including:

- Purchase Order release or modification;
- Purchase Requisition modification or approval;
- Goods Receipt posting or reversal;
- invoice posting, release, modification, or reversal;
- financial posting or reversal;
- configuration or customizing changes;
- master-data changes;
- role or authorization changes.

### Financial or Operational Impact

Human validation is mandatory when the recommended resolution can:

- change a financial posting;
- change inventory;
- change a commercial purchasing commitment;
- change supplier-related data;
- alter approval behavior;
- alter access rights;
- materially affect a production business process.

## AI Actions Before Human Review

Before escalation, the assistant may:

1. summarize the reported issue;
2. identify the relevant SAP MM process;
3. gather available evidence;
4. identify missing information;
5. list evidence-supported hypotheses;
6. recommend read-only diagnostic checks;
7. explain why human validation is required;
8. provide source references to the reviewer.

## AI Actions During Human Review

The assistant may support the reviewer by presenting:

- ticket information;
- SAP read-only context;
- evidence references;
- confidence information generated by the application;
- unresolved questions;
- identified risks.

The assistant must not represent the human review as completed until an authorized reviewer explicitly records a decision through an approved workflow.

## Human Decision Outcomes

A human reviewer may determine that the case should be:

- returned for additional information;
- resolved through an approved operational procedure;
- escalated to another SAP functional team;
- escalated to SAP security/authorization specialists;
- escalated to an MM/FI owner;
- escalated for configuration review;
- closed when the organization-specific process permits it.

## Separation of Recommendation and Execution

The interface and response must distinguish clearly between:

- `AI Recommendation`: diagnostic guidance generated from evidence;
- `Human Decision`: approval or rejection of the proposed course of action;
- `Execution`: an action performed by an authorized SAP user or approved system.

An AI recommendation must never be displayed as an executed action.

## Audit Requirement

When human validation is required, the audit record should capture, where available:

- request identifier;
- reason for HITL;
- evidence used;
- application confidence;
- recommended checks;
- escalation category;
- human-review status.

Production implementations should additionally capture reviewer identity and decision according to organizational security and retention requirements.
